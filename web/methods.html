Methodology and glossary

How Place Lab turns Yelp reviews, Census estimates and public project records into the numbers on the map. This page covers the text and machine-learning methods we used and what the results can and can't support.

1. Research question

Can customer reviews show how areas changed around past public investments, and can that history help a city decide what to build next? We read review text and volume as evidence about local businesses and how people experience them. They don't measure public welfare.

3. Units of analysis

Place Lab uses two units and keeps them separate. Section 5 explains how the time bar changes the period behind the ZIP measures.

  1. ZIP areas. Whole-ZCTA measures comparing 2012–2014 with 2019–2021. They set the map colors and fill the ZIP profile table.
  2. Historical project areas. A near area within 500 m of each project and a comparison ring 1.5–8 km away, observed for two years before and two years after opening. They fill the past-projects table and charts.

The capital-efficiency model in Section 7 used a third unit, a 500 m sample around a fixed point in a ZIP. That result lives in the notebook. The app no longer uses it.

4. Text analysis

We read the review text four ways, each for a different question.

Lexicon sentiment

Every review gets a VADER compound score from −1 to +1. VADER needs no training data and handles negation and intensifiers, which suits short, informal text. It scores the whole review, so it mostly reflects the business experience.

Clause-level topic rules

To separate comments about the area from comments about the business, we split reviews into clauses. Keyword patterns tag nine topics. Each clause gets a target, which is area, business or unclear, and a polarity. Those feed the negative access and negative public-space rates. Section 9 measures how accurate the rules are.

Learned topics

A TF-IDF representation and non-negative matrix factorization learned five topics with no labels supplied. We drew a reproducible sample of 23,691 reviews and fit the topics to the 10,971 from before each opening. Most topics describe food, service and value. That is a finding too. Yelp text says far more about businesses than about public infrastructure.

BERT sentence embeddings and BERTopic

Following Week 6 and Lab 4, the notebook encodes the 413 published place-related excerpts with the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence transformer and fits BERTopic with UMAP, HDBSCAN and c-TF-IDF. Nobody tells it what to look for, and it still finds parking, neighborhood safety, streetcars, construction and trash. The same embeddings feed the BERT-embedding classifier in Section 9.

5. ZIP measures and time periods

Each ZIP measure has a glossary entry with its formula. The main ones are business engagement, change in business engagement, average sentiment, sentiment change vs. the rest of the metro, access & parking discussion, median household income and poverty. Review-based measures need at least 100 reviews per period. Below that, a ZIP shows limited data, which is different from zero.

Choosing the period

The time bar in the page header rebuilds the review-based measures for any quarter or month from January 2012 to December 2021, from monthly ZIP totals of review counts, VADER scores and stars. "One period" shows a single quarter or month, optionally averaged over 3 or 12 months, and compares it with the same period a year earlier so seasons don't masquerade as change. "Compare two" compares two periods of equal length. Sentiment change is always measured against the rest of the metro. Periods of a year or longer need 100 reviews and shorter ones need 50. Access discussion exists by quarter only, so a month that covers no whole quarter shows limited data for it. Income and poverty stay at their ACS baseline. Past-project openings appear on the bar. Choosing one compares up to two years either side of the opening for the project's whole ZIP, which is a much larger area than the 500 m used in Section 6. The default, 2012–2014 against 2019–2021, gives exactly the published values.

6. Project comparison design

For each historical project we compare the change near the project with the change in its comparison ring over the same years. This is a difference-in-differences design. The comparison ring absorbs citywide trends that would have happened anyway, like Yelp's own growth. Ten of the eleven projects had at least 20 reviewed businesses nearby before opening, which is our minimum. Water Works Park did not.

The primary outcome is capital efficiency, the excess reviews near a project per $1 million of reported cost, beyond what the comparison area's growth would predict.

7. Prediction models and evaluation

We chose a ridge regression on three log-transformed, standardized inputs: budget, number of reviewed businesses nearby, and reviews per business. We compared it against a mean-only predictor and three challengers using project- and city-held-out evaluation.

8. Past-project comparison

Place Lab's "What happened around similar past projects?" table lists the observed comparison-adjusted change at each of the ten eligible projects for engagement, sentiment, negative access mentions and negative public-space mentions. You can filter by kind: 4 trails or greenways, 3 waterfront parks, 2 civic parks or plazas and 1 transit line. A family average row sums each kind up.

We tested whether the kind of project predicts its outcome. In a leave-one-project-out check, family averages, partially pooled toward the overall mean, never beat the plain all-project average on any of the four measures. Projects of the same kind differed more than the kinds did. So the table records what happened at specific places. It doesn't estimate what a new project would do.

9. Checking the text measures

The annotation sample has 731 items: 200 random reviews, 200 random keyword-screen clauses and 331 clauses stratified by project, period and topic. Claude Opus labeled each one for target, topics and polarity, seeing only the text. These are agent labels, and a person still needs to recheck them. We then scored the keyword rules, VADER and a BERT-embedding classifier on the same items. The classifier was cross-validated with folds grouped by business.

514 clauses have a decided target and 488 a decided polarity. About 30% of random reviews mention a place at all, with a 95% CI of 25–37%. The keyword screen catches 80% of those. Full tables, including per-topic precision and recall, are in notebook Section 5.5.

For the app, this means area targeting is moderately accurate. The negative-comment rates behind "negative access" and "negative public space" miss most complaints, so Place Lab marks those columns low reliability. The embedding classifier is the clearest fix. Running it on all 1.06 million reviews needs the raw archive, and it's our next step.

10. Talking to Place Lab

A voice or chat assistant can drive Place Lab. The localhost server exposes data queries and page commands through HTTP and a small CLI. The page applies commands using window.placeLab and reports its current view. An assistant can look things up, move around the page and show the user what it found. A planner can say "show me ZIP 19134 in Philly", "go down", "next", "what's north of here?" or "how did trails do?", and the page scrolls, highlights and answers.

GroupToolsWhat they do
Look upget_place_lab_guide, get_zip_profile, find_zips, compare_past_projects, get_project_history, define_termRead-only answers with units, periods, city ranks, reliability notes and definitions
Navigatenavigate (up, down, left, right, in, out), go_to, move_to_neighbor (north, south, east, west), describe_screenUp and down move between sections. Left and right step through ZIPs, measures, project kinds or charts. The page scrolls smoothly and keyboard focus follows.
Showshow_on_map, set_map_time, show_past_projectsSelect a city, ZIP, map layer, map period, project kind, project or chart on screen

The tools call the same code as the tables, so an assistant reports exactly the numbers on screen. Every result has a one-sentence say line for voice. The same line shows as an on-screen caption and goes to a screen-reader live region. Errors say how to recover and list the valid values. Navigation and show tools only change the view. No tool forecasts a new project. The local command server carries view changes and results; the research data stay on this computer. Users who prefer reduced motion get instant jumps instead of smooth scrolling.

To try it, open the browser console and run await placeLab.call("show_on_map", {city: "Philly", zip: "19134"}), then await placeLab.call("navigate", {direction: "down"}).

12. Limitations

  • Ten eligible projects is a small sample, however many reviews they contain.
  • Reviews measure engagement with Yelp-listed businesses, not visits, revenue or resident wellbeing.
  • Reported project costs differ in public and private scope.
  • Text accuracy figures rest on agent labels that a person has not yet rechecked. The keyword rules undercount negative comments.
  • The later ZIP window (2019–2021) includes COVID-19, and some post-opening periods include 2020. The time bar marks COVID from March 2020.
  • Periods shorter than a year need only 50 reviews, so single quarters and months are noisier than the default three-year comparison.
  • Income and poverty estimates are historical baselines with sampling error.
  • Nothing here shows that a project caused a change.
